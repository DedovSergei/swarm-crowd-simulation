The venues, the stair-flow calibration, the experiment matrix (mean and spread, with the stair-flow bracket), the comparison with the model before
the calibration, what governs the evacuation time, the key findings, the reproducibility on one driver, the gridlock robustness, the performance

a committed run or report named below. Values cited from the literature are as reported in the named source and not yet checked against the original.

## The stair-flow calibration

Without a calibration the model's gangways are about 1.8x as permissive as stair planning flows: the incline speed slows the walkers to stair speeds,
but they keep their level-ground spacing. The calibration sets the spacing on the inclines; nothing else in the model changes.

| target (per metre of the gangway's 1.20 m clear width; as reported in the sources named, not yet checked against the originals) | ascending | descending | source |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **EMPIRICAL** (the default) | 1.22 persons/(m s) = 73.2 persons/(m min) | 1.25 = 75.0 | Burghardt, Seyfried & Klingsch (2013), Transportation Research Part C 37: the measured maximum specific flows on stairs |
| **CONSERVATIVE** (the bracket) | 1.10 = 66 | 1.10 = 66 | Guide to Safety at Sports Grounds (Green Guide), 6th ed. (2018): the stepped-surface rate (the venues' exits were sized with it) |

Measured stair flows spread below EMPIRICAL (as reported in Fruin 1971: 62-66, and the SFPE correlation: 45-52 per clear metre of a 1.20 m gangway; not
yet checked against the originals): EMPIRICAL is the optimistic end of the measured range. **The rule:** on an incline the headway time gap is T(g) = T_level + (T_stair -
T_level) s(min(|g| / tan 27 deg, 1)), the incline speed's smoothstep of the slope g along the travel direction; level floors keep T_level =
0.30 s bit for bit. **Calibrated** with the stair harness (an inclined periodic corridor at a gangway's width and rake 0.5; the stationary
specific flow over density, three seeds; its maximum): T_stair = 0.625 s up / 0.65 s down (EMPIRICAL), 0.75 / 0.78 s (CONSERVATIVE). The
calibration ran on AMD 26.8.1 (results/validation/stair_flow/calibration_amd_26.8.1); re-run on 26.10.1 the stair harness gives the same maxima to three decimals.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| stair harness, ascending [persons/(m s)] | 1.902 | **1.220** (1.000x the target) | 1.093 (0.99x) |
| stair harness, descending | 1.952 | **1.260** (1.008x) | 1.094 (0.99x) |
| in situ: single-outlet straight gangways (M, L), saturated 10 s windows [persons/(m min)] | 94.0 | **71.6** (0.98x the ascending target) | - |
| in situ: all straight gangways pooled (the gangway bound's flow) [persons/(m min)] | 121.1 | 94.5 | - |

Stair harness: results/validation/stair_flow/harness/ (fd_harness at b6a7874). Unchanged by the calibration: the level-ground fundamental diagram

## What governs t100 (bottleneck analysis)

Lower bounds (results/evacuation/matrix/bottleneck.md): (a) exits: N / (total exit width x the doors' measured capacity, 83.3
persons/(m min): S's doors at 100% before the calibration, where they run at capacity; a door is a level passage, unchanged); (b) gangways:
seated N / (total gangway width x the model's sustained gangway flow, 94.5 persons/(m min) on straight gangways; before the calibration:
121.1); (c) travel: the longest unimpeded walk from a seat. t_lb = max(a, b, c).

| venue | (a) exits | (b) gangways | (c) travel | t_lb | t100 | **efficiency t100 / t_lb** (before the calibration) | people per straight gangway: max / mean | t100 if each row segment split its seats between its two end gangways to balance them | **verdict** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| S | 83 s | **164 s** | 27 s | 164 s | 238.2 s | **1.45** (1.49) | 1.00 (corner gangways: 4 people each) | (187.5 s, needs the corner gangways) | **DESIGN** |
| M | 203 s | **299 s** | 52 s | 299 s | 563.4 s | **1.88** (1.98) | 1.82 | 457.1 s (-19%) | **gangway DESIGN + ROUTING** (40% of the excess) |
| L | 369 s | **763 s** | 103 s | 763 s | 1 477.4 s | **1.94** (2.02) | 1.66 | 1 135.0 s (-23%) | **gangway DESIGN + ROUTING** (48% of the excess) |

- **The verdicts hold before and after the calibration, on one driver.** The gangway bound binds in every case; the stair spacing
  lowered the gangway capacity and lengthened t100 together, so the efficiencies changed by only 3-5% (S 1.49 -> 1.45, M 1.98 -> 1.88,
  L 2.02 -> 1.94).
- **S is governed by its design.** Its 8 straight gangways carry equal loads (464 people each) and run saturated for 80% of their
  people-weighted active time; with their own measured flow (118.4 persons/(m min), each with two outlets) they bound t100 at 196 s, 0.82x
  t100 (the rest: the start before they fill, and the tail). The 4 corner gangways carry 4 people each because their heads reach concourse
  corners without doors. The doors run at 0.77 of their capacity.
- **M and L are governed by the gangways, with a large routing share.** t100 is about twice the bound; the doors run at 0.52 (M) and 0.29
  (L) of their capacity. The straight gangways' loads differ by a factor of 1.7-1.8 (max / mean): the first gangway of every wing takes the
  dead-end segments' seats and the most traffic. A split of each row segment between its two end gangways that balanced their service times
  would cut t100 by 19% (M) and 23% (L), 40% and 48% of the excess over the bound; the rest is design (the dead-end segments only gangway 0
  can serve, the corner gangways and their merges, the walk to and from the slowest gangway).
- **The route choice does not consider gangway congestion (confirmed limitation).** The queue-aware exit choice balances the exits (no exit
  width unused; doors and avenues used in every venue) but not the gangways: its estimate counts the queue at the exit, not the occupancy of the
  gangway on the way.
- **The uninformed crowd (nearest exit) is governed by routing:** t100 +52% (S), +52% (M), +33% (L) against the default, the avenues
  unused; a balancing split would recover 34% (M) and 31% (L).
- **Gangway flow vs stair guidance:** a single-outlet straight gangway carries 71.6 persons/(m min) in its saturated windows (0.98x the
  measured stair maximum, 1.08x the Green Guide's 66; both as reported, not yet checked against the originals); the pooled straight-gangway flow is 94.5 because gangway 0 discharges at both
  ends. Gangway-governed t100 correspond to the measured stair maxima, the optimistic end of measured stair flows; the planning rate adds
  5-8% (the bracket).

## Key findings

1. Evacuation time at full occupancy grows much faster than capacity: S 238.2 s (4.0 min), M 563.4 s (9.4 min), L 1 477.4 s (24.6 min) for
   3 728, 15 832 and 63 424 seats. **With calibrated stair flow, M exceeds the 8-minute planning reference its exits were sized for (480 s,
   as reported; not yet checked against the original) by 17% (before the calibration: 461.1 s, below it), while S stays well below it (0.50x); L is at 3.1x.**
2. Calibrating the gangways' stair flow lengthens every full-occupancy evacuation by about a quarter (S +25%, M +22%, L +23%). The planning
   rate instead of the measured maxima adds 5-8%: the stair-flow bracket is S 238-254 s, M 563-591 s, L 1 477-1 589 s.
3. In all three bowls the gangways, not the exits, set t100: the gangway bound exceeds the exit-width bound 1.5-2.1x, and the doors run at
   29-77% of their capacity. The total-exit-width rule does not guarantee egress in deep single-tier bowls without vomitories or tiers.
4. In the large bowls about a fifth of t100 is lost to uneven gangway loading that a gangway-aware route choice could remove (M -19%, L -23%).
   The rest is the layout: dead-end row segments, corner gangways and their merges.
5. Overcrowding: S at 125% / 150% evacuates 7% / 22% slower than at 100%, and its exposure to critical density rises 1.2x / 2.3x. People
   standing on the concourse block the gangway heads (the straight gangways' saturated discharge falls from 2.37 to 1.89 persons/s per gangway
   at 150%). M and L cannot take 125%: their walkway and concourse hold at most 21% / 9% more people at 2.5 persons/m^2; at that maximum t100
   rises 5% (M) and 3% (L).
6. **People standing on the gangways as well (S 150%) change t100 by +3% but lower the exposure to critical density in circulation areas by
   17%. The cause is the concourse occupancy, not a metric artefact:** the gangway variant places 408-436 of the 1 864 standing people on the
   gangways, so 23% fewer stand on the concourse; the concourse's person-seconds fall 26% and its exposure halves (-50%), because exposure counts
   only person-steps at or above 5 persons/m^2 and the crowd at the gangway heads crosses that threshold much less often (a threshold effect);
   the gangways carry 5% more person-seconds but 7% less exposure (they discharge into a less crowded concourse). The small exit-passage cells
   contribute about the same in both variants (820-909 person-s).
7. Without congestion-aware exit choice (nearest exit), t100 rises by 33-52% and the exposure to critical density 1.5-3.0x; the avenues stay
   empty.
8. The results are reproducible to a few percent: three seeds at 95% load span 0.9-5.8% of t100 (t50 and t90 within 1%); none of the 41 runs
   stalled. **On another GPU driver the same runs differ like a rounding change** (results/evacuation/matrix/driver_effect.md): the default
   model's t100 by -4.1 .. +2.1% (mean |change| 1.0% over 30 runs), t50 and t90 within 2%. The model before the calibration is more sensitive
   in its tails (t100 -4.6 .. +15.6%, one gridlock in 30 runs), which is part of why the stair spacing is the default.
9. The model's level corridor fundamental diagram matches Weidmann's within RMS 0.126 m/s (max flow 80 vs 74 persons/(m min), +9%; figure validation_fundamental_diagram); the
   gangway stair reproduces the measured stair maxima within 1% in the stair harness and within 2% in situ (single-outlet gangways); every
   run satisfies the hard-body and venue constraints with no exemption.
10. **Narrow passages (no model change; figure validation_corridor_width_flow):** the model's level specific flow depends on the boundary. Between full-height walls (the
    calibration's corridor) the maximum is 1.33-1.34 persons/(m s) at 1.2, 2.4 and 3.0 m (a 1.2 m corridor jams from 2 persons/m^2): the
    torso-level obstacle avoidance keeps agents off the walls (-26.3% at 3 m against low walls). Between low walls (0.85 m) it is 1.82-1.97 per
    wall-to-wall metre, rising only 8% from 3.0 to 1.2 m (lane quantisation with 0.40 m bodies: a narrow corridor holds whole lanes). The
    venues' 1.20 m concourse doors (full-height sides) carry 83.3 persons/(m min) = 1.39 persons/(m s) at capacity, the full-walled corridor's
    maximum; their mean flows with the calibrated gangways are 63.8 (S), 43.3 (M), 23.8 (L), fed below capacity. The stair harness's level
    control (rake 0) reached 2.5 persons/(m s) at the gangway's width because of its low walls and its normalisation by the 1.20 m centre span
    (1.90 per wall-to-wall metre), not because the passage is narrow.
11. **Gridlock robustness:** at the heaviest loads (S 150%, M 121.4%, L 109.3%, M 90-94%) the default model finished all 31 runs with
    no stall (the longest pause 24.3 s); with the matrix's runs, 0 stalls in 52 distinct runs. The one gridlock on record belongs to the model
    before the calibration: a crossing of two streams at a gangway merge that the exit choice does not resolve.
12. **Performance:** the GPU simulates every venue at full occupancy at 45-258x real time (0.68-3.93 ms per step for 3 728-63 424
    agents); its lead over the CPU reference at 20 threads grows with the crowd from 3-7x (S) to 17-50x (L), and the terrace's stress sweep
    reaches 191x at 262 144 agents, still 1.4x faster than real time. The hard-body projection dominates the step on the terrace (83-96%); on the
    venues the navigation and venue code of the predict pass is as large.

its 26.8.1 counterpart (driver_effect.md):

## Gridlock robustness

The matrix has one permanent gridlock, in the model before the stair calibration (M at 121.4%, seed 2). This check measures how often the
**default model** gridlocks or stalls at the heaviest loads (results/evacuation/gridlock_robustness/, gridlock.md; run_experiment at 15aa31e on
AMD 26.10.1.261008, every per-step check). A *pause* is an interval without an evacuation while agents remain (in an unfinished run, also from the
last evacuation to the end); **stall** = the longest pause > 60 s; **gridlock** = not finished at the step limit (40 000 steps = 6 996 s).

| venue, load | runs (seeds) | finished | gridlocks | stalls (> 60 s) | longest pause: max / median [s] | t100 mean (min-max) [s] |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| S, 150% (5 592 agents) | 10 (1-10) | 10 | **0** | **0** | 5.2 / 3.6 | 291.8 (282.1-298.2) |
| M, 121.4% (19 223, the floor's maximum) | 10 (1-10) | 10 | **0** | **0** | 2.6 / 1.4 | 588.7 (581.7-596.3) |
| M, 90-94% (seeded partial loads) | 5 (4-8) | 5 | **0** | **0** | 1.4 / 1.4 | 520.2 (508.1-534.3) |
| L, 109.3% (69 311, the floor's maximum) | 6 (4-9) | 6 | **0** | **0** | 24.3 / 2.2 | 1 561.3 (1 506.5-1 784.9) |
| **all** | **31** | **31** | **0** | **0** | 24.3 | |

- **The default model gridlocked in 0 of 31 runs and stalled in none**; with the matrix's 27 default-model runs on the same driver and code (no
  stall, longest pause 8.8 s): **0 of 52 distinct runs**, which bounds a per-run stall probability to below ~6% (95%, rule of three). Every
  run passes every per-step check. The runs that repeat a matrix spawn reproduce it byte for byte.
- One slow tail: L seed 9 took 1 784.9 s against 1 506.5-1 526.1 s for seeds 4-8 (t50 and t90 within 1%): its last 200 agents needed 293 s
  (seed 4: 63 s), with pauses of up to 24.3 s, below the stall definition. Re-run with stall snapshots: a counterflow on one corner gangway
  (at 1 540 s 91 of the last 135 agents on it, 84 heading one way and 6 the other, the queue creeping at 0.13 m/s) that dissolved within
  ~60 s, then the last agents' climb at the stair speed (results/evacuation/gridlock_diagnosis/): the same mechanism as the gridlock, short-lived.
- **The mechanism of the one gridlock on record** (the model before the calibration; re-run with stall snapshots:
  results/evacuation/gridlock_diagnosis/): at the top of a quadrant the corner gangway merges with the last straight gangway of a wing
  below their two doors. Agents climbing the corner gangway towards the straight gangway's door and agents on the straight gangway bound for
  the corner door cross in opposite directions in that junction (11 of the 15 opposing pairs, the nearest in contact); bodies cannot pass
  each other, the headway rule stops every agent with another in its lane, and the exit choice does not dissolve it: **0 of the 120 agents
  switched exit in 480 s**, because every door is free and the walking distances favour each agent's assigned door. The choice balances exits,
  not gangways or junctions, and cannot see that the route to its best exit is blocked.
- **Limitation for the thesis**: no passing and no awareness of a blocked route. The default model's calibrated stair flow did not meet the
  configuration in 52 runs, but the mechanism is unchanged, so a gridlock remains possible in other spawns or layouts. Remedies (proposed, not
  implemented): a junction-consistent exit choice (each junction's outflow to one door, or a counterflow cost in E), a deadlock release (an
  agent stopped for some seconds behind an opposing one re-chooses with the blocked exit excluded), passing on 1.20 m gangways (a model change
  with a stair re-calibration), or a layout without merged gangway heads.

## Performance: the thesis benchmark

The simulation's cost on the GPU and on the CPU reference with the current model (the default model of everything above: hard bodies with the
projection, the navigation field, the queue-aware exit choice, the incline speed, the stair spacing), from **one benchmark run**:
results/benchmarks/thesis/thesis_20261023_2009_cells.csv (benchmark_crowd built at **2460e1c**, dirty 0; tables: perf_tables.md there, generated by
tools/figures/perf_tables.py). Measurements of the point / disk model before the hard bodies, on the previous driver, appear only as the evidence of
what each GPU optimisation gained (the optimisation chain below).

**Methodology.** AMD Radeon RX 7800 XT, OpenGL 4.3 Core, driver **26.10.1.261008**; the GPU clock fixed in AMD Adrenalin (manual tuning, minimum
2 500 MHz, maximum 2 620 MHz; clock note "adrenalin manual min2500 max2620"); Intel Core i5-13600KF (6 P-cores with 2 threads each + 8 E-cores = 20
logical), Windows power throttling switched off for the benchmark process; Release build (MSVC /O2, /fp:precise, OpenMP 2.0). The quiet-GPU rule:
the run started after 60 s without other GPU use (tools/benchmark/gpuwatch.ps1), nothing else ran during it, other processes' GPU use was logged
every 10 s (mean 0.12%, max 5.75% in one sample). **Protocol:** 5 rounds; every round starts with a reference probe (GPU, the
terrace at N = 4 096, spawn, 300 steps; its coefficient of variation across the rounds is the stability figure: **0.99%**), then runs every
(workload, state, implementation) cell once, in an order rotated between rounds; GPU warm-up >= 200 steps and >= 2 s per cell, then 300 measured
steps (100 at N = 262 144); CPU warm-up up to 20 steps, measured 100 steps where cheap and fewer at large N (CPU-1 at L: 5 steps in 3 rounds; at
the 262 144 stress point: 1 step in 1 round, 3.6-4.7 min per step), recorded per row. **Snapshot mode**: every timed step starts from the identical
agent state, restored outside the timed region (a venue's exit choice runs on with its own 3 s rhythm). States: *spawn* (every seat occupied;
the terrace's spawner) and *congested* (a venue's GPU state at t50 of its 100% run; the terrace's after 250 steps). GPU times are GPU-execution
times (GL_TIMESTAMP per pass, read back after the measured loop); CPU times steady_clock. **The safety metrics are off** (the simulation alone,
as in production benchmarking). Self-check before measuring: every golden pin of the terrace, S, M and L (GPU and CPU) matched. Cell value: the
median over rounds of the per-round median ms per step; the real-time factor = simulated time per wall time = DT_real (174.9 ms) / ms per step.

**Findings.**
1. At constant density the GPU step grows far slower than the crowd: 17x the agents from S to L cost the GPU 2.7x (spawn) and 3.1x (congested),
   20 CPU threads 18x / 16x. The GPU's lead over 20 CPU threads therefore grows with the venue: 7.4x (S) to 50x (L) at spawn, 3.2x to 17x
   congested.
2. **The GPU simulates every venue at full occupancy faster than real time by 45x or more** (L congested: 45x; S spawn: 258x); 20 CPU threads
   manage 1.9-2.6x at L, one thread is slower than real time from M on (M congested 0.91x, L 0.15-0.21x).
3. The terrace's stress sweep at the current model's cost: GPU vs 20 threads 4.2x (1 024) to 191x (262 144); the
   physically impossible 262 144-agent crowd still runs 1.4x faster than real time on the GPU.
4. The projection dominates the terrace's step on both processors; on the venues the GPU spends more in the predict pass (navigation and venue
   code) than in the projection when congested, the CPU still in the projection.
5. 20 CPU threads are 6-15x faster than one (9-13x on the venues; the P-/E-core mix: 11.9x at M congested), so the GPU vs single-thread factor is
   30-640x on the venues (S congested to L spawn) and 2 300-2 700x at the stress point.

## Known limitations

- **Single-tier bowls without vomitories:** the generator builds one tier; real large stadia shorten the gangway catchments with vomitories
  (tunnels to the concourse at mid-height) or several tiers. The finding that the gangways govern t100 in M and L is a finding about this layout.
- **Static exit set:** every exit is always open; no blocked or closed exits, no staged or late alarm, no pre-movement (reaction) time,
  homogeneous agents (no age or fitness mix).
- **Route choice ignores gangway congestion** (confirmed above), its walking term assumes the level speed on stairs, and one specific flow J
  is used for doors and avenues.
- **Stairs as inclines with calibrated flow:** gangways are continuous 26.6 deg ramps walked at stair speeds (0.58 m/s up, 0.655 down; as reported in Fruin 1971 via Fujiyama & Tyler 2004, not yet checked against the originals),
  and their spacing is calibrated so that a gangway-like corridor's maximum sustained flow equals the measured stair maxima (EMPIRICAL, the
  optimistic end of measured stair flows) or the planning rate (CONSERVATIVE). Treads and risers, the stepping gait, handrails and overtaking
  on stairs are not modelled; the calibration fixes a corridor maximum, and the in-situ flow depends on how the gangway is fed (single-outlet
  gangways reach 0.98x the target; gangway 0, with two outlets, carries about twice as much per metre).
- **No passing in single-file seat rows or against a stream on a gangway, and an exit choice that cannot see a blocked route:** the exit choice
  can assign neighbours to exits in opposite directions; they then block each other until the hysteresis lets one switch, or for good. With the
  model before the calibration this ended in a permanent gridlock in 1 of 30 runs on 26.10.1 (M 121.4%, seed 2: two streams crossing where a
  corner gangway merges with a straight one below their doors; 0 exit switches in 480 s; on 26.8.1 the same spawn stalled for 118 s). The
  default model gridlocked and stalled in **0 of 52 distinct runs** (31 runs at the heaviest loads and the matrix's 27), a per-run
  probability below ~6% at 95%, but the mechanism remains (section "Gridlock robustness"; remedies proposed there).
- **Area-normalised density in seat rows:** a 0.50 m clearway holds a single-file queue, and its 1 m cells' density is not comparable with
  Fruin's thresholds. The headline density metrics cover the circulation zones only, and the seat rows are reported apart.
- **Level flow depends on the boundary** (key finding 10): the level calibration to Weidmann was made between full-height walls; a level
  passage between boundaries lower than about 0.98 m (the torso sample's reach) would carry up to ~1.4x the calibrated flow per metre. On the
  venues only the gangways have such low sides, and their flow is set by the stair calibration; not recalibrated.
- **Overcrowding model:** standing people are placed uniformly at random (the 2.5 persons/m^2 cap is a mean; 1 m cells start at up to 4.2
  persons/m^2, below the crush threshold), only on the walkway and concourse (or also the gangways); M and L cannot reach 125% under that cap.
- **Planning references** (Green Guide rates, the 8-minute egress time, Fruin's levels of service, the crush threshold, Weidmann's diagram,
  the stair speeds and the stair maxima) are cited from secondary sources: as reported there, not yet checked against the originals. The terrace is exempt from INV-row and from the
  venue-geometry overlap limits (its bare treads); the seated venues have no exemption.
- **GPU driver:** results are pinned to one driver (AMD 26.10.1.261008); another driver changes GPU results like a different rounding
  (Reproducibility above). On 26.10.1 the GPU's band and cell bounds of the analytic venues differ from the CPU mirror by one ulp (the
  compiler reassociates constant sums; results/validation/navigation/S/gpu_cpu_goal_diff): GPU / CPU agreement is checked with a rounding-aware criterion.
- **CPU reference** cross-checks: S with the stair spacing (CPU step 1361 against the GPU's 1362 on 26.10.1), M (before the stair calibration: 3.2%, results/validation/cpu_gpu_equivalence/M_load100_cpu_20261012_1947 and M_load100_gpu_20261012_1923); L's CPU
  reference was not run (hours).
